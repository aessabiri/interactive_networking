 Chapter 5 – Networking Fundamentals 
 
What is a Network 
It is useful to distinguish between a physical device and a network node. 
A network node is any physical or virtual endpoint or intermediary that participates in network 
communication.  Examples  include  computers,  servers,  printers,  routers,  switches,  Virtual 
Machines, and virtual network appliances. 
In this material, the term physical device refers specifically to hardware, while a network node 
may be physical or virtual.

Domain vs Computer Workgroup 
 
 
Workgroup vs Domain 
 
A Windows Workgroup is a peer-to-peer arrangement in which each computer manages its 
own local user accounts, security settings, and shared resources. 
No  computer  centrally  controls  the  other  computers  in  the  Workgroup.  Access  to  a  shared 
folder, printer, or other resource depends on the accounts and permissions configured on the 
computer that provides the resource. 
A Windows Domain is a centrally managed environment in which user accounts, computers, 
security policies, and access to network resources can be administered through services such 
as Active Directory Domain Services. 
Domains    are    commonly    used    in    organizations    because    they    provide    centralized 
authentication, authorization, policy management, and administration.

Network Types 
 
Local Area Network (LAN) 
 
 
A LAN Network 
 
A Local Area Network, or LAN, connects devices within a limited geographical area, such as a 
home, office, school, building, or campus. 
A computer used by an employee or another end user may be called a workstation. 
However, a LAN may also contain servers, printers, switches, routers, Access Points, phones, 
cameras, and other network devices. 
A LAN can operate with or without a dedicated server. Devices may connect through wired 
Ethernet, wireless technology, or a combination of both.  

Tsounias Ioannis  4 
Wide Area Network (WAN) 
 
 
A WAN Network 
 
WAN links  often  have higher  latency than  LAN  connections  because  traffic  may travel over 
longer distances and pass through service-provider infrastructure. 
However, the available bandwidth, reliability, and overall performance depend on the type of 
WAN service, the provider’s infrastructure,  the  geographical  distance,  and  the  network 
design. Modern WAN services can provide very high transmission speeds.

Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN) 
 
 
A WLAN Network 
 
A Wireless Local Area Network, or WLAN, is a LAN that uses wireless technology, normally Wi-
Fi,  to  connect  compatible  devices. Wireless  clients  commonly  connect  through  an Access 
Point. The Access Point normally bridges wireless devices to the wired LAN. 
Internet  access,  when  available,  is  normally  provided  through  a  router  or  another  Default 
Gateway.  A  WLAN  can  exist  and  support  local  communication  even  when  no  Internet 
connection is available. 
One major advantage of wireless networking is mobility. Users can move within the coverage 
area while maintaining their network connection.

Internet 
 
 
 
The  Internet  is  a  global  system  of  interconnected  networks  that  communicate  by  using  the 
TCP/IP protocol suite. 
It depends on physical infrastructure such as routers, switches, servers, data centers, copper 
and  fiber-optic  cables,  wireless  links,  and  submarine  cables.  However,  it  also  depends  on 
logical  addressing,  routing,  protocols,  and  services  that  allow  independent  networks  to 
operate together. 
For this reason, the Internet is commonly described as a network of networks. 
The Internet supports many services such as: 
• World Wide Web: it’s a library consisting of files, documents, videos, pictures etc. that 
can be accessed over the internet. 
• eMail: It’s a service that let us send and receive messages over the Internet. 
• Social Media: They are online platforms/applications that allow users to interact with 
each other. 
• Search Engines: It’s the tool that helps us to find information on the Internet.

Intranet – Extranet 
 
Intranet 
 
An Intranet is a private network or collection of internal services used by an organization. 
It allows authorized users to access internal information, applications, documents, and other 
organizational  resources.  Access  may  be  controlled  through  authentication,  permissions, 
network-security controls, and organizational policies. 
An Intranet may or may not provide access to the public Internet. 
Extranet 
 
An Extranet is a controlled extension of an organization’s internal environment that provides 
selected  external  users,  such  as  customers,  suppliers,  or  business partners,  with  access  to 
specific  resources. Access  may  be  provided  through  a VPN,  a  secure  web  portal,  an 
authenticated application, or another protected access method. 
External  users  should  receive  access  only  to  the  resources  required  for  their  approved 
purpose.

Data Transmission Technologies 
 
Asymmetric Digital Subscriber Line (ADSL) 
ADSL and ADSL2+ transmit data through existing copper telephone lines. They are asymmetric 
technologies, meaning that the downstream and upstream speeds are different. 
ADSL2+  can  provide  better  performance  than  earlier  ADSL  implementations.  However,  DSL 
technologies  are  increasingly  considered  legacy  access  solutions,  although they  may  still  be 
used in locations where newer infrastructure is unavailable.

Very high-speed Digital Subscriber Line (VDSL)

than ADSL over suitable line conditions. 
Performance  is  strongly  affected by the distance between the customer and the provider’s 
equipment,  the  quality  of  the  copper  line,  electromagnetic  interference,  crosstalk,  and  the 
technology profile  being used. Performance  normally decreases as the copper-line  distance

Protocols and Protocol Suite 
 
Network communication is governed by agreed rules called protocols. A protocol defines how 
information is formatted, addressed, transmitted, received, and interpreted. It may also define 
timing,  sequencing,  error  handling,  and  the  actions  that  communicating  systems  must 
perform. 
Different protocols support different functions. For example, FTP is one protocol that may be 
used to transfer files between systems, although other file-transfer methods also exist. 
A   protocol   suite   is   a   collection   of   related   protocols   that   work   together   to   support 
communication   between   systems.   A   protocol   suite   does   not   guarantee   that   every 
communication will be error-free; reliability depends on the protocols and mechanisms being 
used. 
In  the  field  of  IT  there  are  two  major  implementation  models.  The  TCP/IP  (Transmission 
Control  Protocol/Internet  Protocol)  model  and  the  OSI  (Open  Systems  Interconnection) 
model.  Very  important  to  know  is  that  we  implement  TCP/IP  rules,  but  when  we  have  to 
troubleshoot or refer to a specific portion of the IT infrastructure, we follow the OSI terms. 
The network IT infrastructure is divided in Layers. The OSI model, which is also called 
as OSI Reference model, it consists of seven layers and is far more analytic than TCP/IP 
which has only four Layers.

Network Devices 
 
Hub (Layer 1 device) 
 
The HUB is  an  older  network  device  that  is  rarely  used  in  modern  networks,  it  is  used  to 
connect  many  network  devices  such  as  computers  printers  within  a  local  LAN  network.  Its 
mode of operation was quite simple, when a data packet was arriving at one of its ports, the 
HUB  forwarded  (Broadcasted)  the  traffic  out  of  all  its  remaining active ports,  without 
examining  the  packet  for  errors,  checking  the  sender  or  the  receiver.  This  means  that  all 
devices  connected  to  the  hub  would  receive  and  process, up  to  a  specific  level,  the  packet 
regardless of who the recipient was. 
This caused major problems and delays in communication because all devices connected to 
the Hub were part of a Collision Domain. That is, if two devices were sending data at the same 
time, there was a high probability that conflicts would occur during transmission. 
 
 
How the Hub Handles Traffic 

Tsounias Ioannis  17 
To manage to mitigate the problem they introduced a mechanism new for the time, CSMA/CD 
(Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection). 
But how did this mechanism work? 
• The sending device was monitoring the transmission medium to see if it was idle and 
so to begin to send data. 
• Multiple devices can access the medium but only one can send data at a time. 
• When  two  devices  were  sending  data  in  parallel  then  a  collision  would  occur.  The 
devices would then detect the collision and stop the transmission. 
• Finally,  after  the  collision,  a  random  clock  was  run  for  each  device  before  trying  to 
send data again. 
The  collision  problem  has  been  minimized  by  the  extensive  use  of  switches  in  modern 
networks. A Switch creates a separate collision domain for each connected device.

Switch (Layer 2 device & Layer 3 device) 
 
A  switch  is  a  very  basic  network  device.  It  is  used  like  the  hub  device  to connect  several 
computers or other network devices to a local network. Its main job is to receive a data frame 
from  the  sender  and  forward  it  to  the  appropriate  final  recipient.  However,  the  operation 
mode of the switch is completely different from that of the hub. When a data packet arrives 
at the switch port, after checking some specific characteristics of the packet, it forwards it to 
the specific port to which the recipient is connected. 
The switch checks the MAC Address of the frame and compares it with the MAC Address Table 
stored internally. So, it can check if there is a record with the recipient's details and forward 
the packet out the appropriate port. 
 
 
Network Devices Connected on a Switch 
In order for a switch to build its internal MAC Address Table, it must follow a process called 
MAC Learning. So, when a Switch receives an Ethernet frame on one of its ports, it examines 
the MAC Address of the frame and if it finds that there is no corresponding entry, it registers 
it together with the number of the port that received the frame. Each of these records has a 
particular lifetime (Aging Time). If the switch does not receive any traffic from a specific MAC 
within  a  certain  time,  then  this  record  is  deleted.  It  is  a  basic  process  of  the  Switch,  which 
allows  it  to  forward  packets  only  to  the  appropriate  ports  and  thus  manages  to  reduce 
unnecessary traffic within the network.

Switches can operate in three different modes to forward traffic 
 
i. Store and Forward 
In this mode the switch stores temporarily the whole frame and investigates the Frame Check 
Sequence,  and  if  it doesn’t find any problem it forwards it the receiver. In case there is an 
error, the switch drops the frame. 
 
Store n Forward Method 
ii. Cut Through 
Different mode of operation from Store and Forward, and considerably faster. In this mode 
the switch only examines the recipient's MAC Address and starts forwarding the frame to it 
without waiting to receive the entire message. 
 
Cut-Through Method

Router (Layer 3 Device) 
 
A  router  is  a  network  device  that  is  responsible  for  sending  and  receiving  data  between 
different networks. It chooses to which network and through which path to send the data and 
joins different networks and subnets. Within a LAN, if a device wants to communicate with a 
different network than its own, such as the Internet, then all it needs to know is the “Default 
Gateway” of the network it belongs to. 
Another basic function of the Router is to divide the network into “Broadcast Domains”. Each 
router  port  is  also  a  separate  Broadcast  Domain.  A  Broadcast  Domain  includes  all  network 
devices that can communicate with each other at OSI Layer 2 via broadcasting. 
 
One Router - Two Networks 
The router, like the switches, creates an address table called “Routing  Table”. This table 
contains  all  the  IP  addresses  of  the  networks  that  the  router  knows,  either  statically 
(Manually) or dynamically (Routing Protocol), so that it can route the traffic.

Cabling 
 
In networks, data transfer is either wired or wireless. In this paragraph we will talk about wired 
networks.  The  most  common  and  widely  used  cables  are  twisted  pair  copper  cables.  The 
cables are twisted to neutralize electromagnetic interference (Crosstalk). 
Cable Types 
 
Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP) cable: It consists of four pairs of cables. It comes in two forms, 
one is rigid because it has a plastic core inside, and so it goes more easily through channels 
and  walls.  There  is  also the classic  cable which  is  flexible  and  only  has  copper wires  inside. 
Each pair has one colored wire and one with the corresponding color but with a white stripe. 
Depending  on  the  company  that  produces  them,  the  cables  are  inside  a  PVC  plastic.  Their 
termination is always on an RJ-45 connector. 
Shielded  Twisted  Pair  (STP) cable:  Similar  to  UTP  but  with  additional  shielding  to  further 
reduce EMI, used in environments with high electromagnetic interference. 
Foil  Shielded  Twisted  Pair  (FTP) cable:  Uses  a thin  layer  of  aluminum  foil  to  provide  100% 
coverage of the conductors it surrounds. 
Braided  Shielded  cable:  Uses  a  mesh  of  woven  copper  wires  to  provide  shielding.  Offers 
flexibility and durability. 
Of course, there are other types of shielded cables which are a combination of the others such 
as: S/FTP, F/FTP, SF/FTP cables.

Layer 1: Physical Layer 
 
It  deals  with  the  physical  connection  between  devices.  As  we  have  already  mentioned  the 
three first layers are containing hardware components also. So, in L1 we can find cables, hubs, 
and other devices. 
Layer 2: Data Link Layer 
 
Provides  node-to-node  data  transfer  and  manages  error  detection  and  correction  from  the 
physical layer. L2 hardware components: Switches, network interface cards (NIC). Protocols: 
Ethernet, PPP (Point-to-Point Protocol). 
In Ethernet, the Data Link Layer uses the Frame Check Sequence to detect frame corruption. 
If  a  frame  is  detected  as  corrupted,  it  is  normally  discarded.  Ethernet  does not correct  the 
damaged frame or automatically request its retransmission. 
Recovery, when required, is provided by another protocol or application mechanism. 
  

Tsounias Ioannis  30 
The Data Link Layer is divided into two sublayers: 
1. Logical Link Control (LLC): It handles error checking and frame synchronization. 
It provides a way to identify the network layer protocol being used. 
2. Media  Access  Control  (MAC):  It controls  how  devices  on  the  network  gain 
access to the medium and permission to transmit data. It deals with Physical 
Addressing and network topology. 
Each  device  within  a  network  has  a  unique  MAC  Address  which  is  used  for  device 
Identification  at  L2.  MAC  Addresses  are  48-bit  long  and  are  assigned  on  NICs.  The 
Ethernet protocol is the most important L2 Protocol. It is also known as IEEE 802.3, is 
a set of rules that enable devices to communicate in a LAN environment. 
A very important feature of L2 is VLAN (Virtual Local Area Network), which allows for 
network  segmentation  improving  network  security  and  performance.  In  Data  Link 
Layer  we can apply the Link  Aggregation  feature,  which  combines  multiple  network 
connection to increase bandwidth and provide redundancy. Moreover, in L2 we can 
configure QoS in order to prioritize specific traffic to increase performance. 
VLAN most important characteristics: 
o Segmentation: A  VLAN  can  divide  a  network  into  smaller  ones  and  thus  create 
separate  Broadcast  Domains.  This  means  that  devices  from  different  VLANs  cannot 
communicate directly to each other without a layer 3 device, like a router or a layer 3 
switch. 
o Flexibility: By using VLANs an administrator is able to group network devices  based 
on function, or by department, regardless of their physical location. 
o Traffic Management: With VLANs an administrator can isolate the traffic and improve 
performance by reducing congestion. 
o Security: VLANs provide logical segmentation and create separate broadcast domains. 
However,  a  VLAN  alone  does  not  enforce  complete  access  control.  Communication 
between  VLANs  should  be  controlled  through  routing  policies,  Access  Control  Lists, 
firewalls, authentication, and other security controls. 
o VLAN   Tagging:   VLANs   for   the   purpose   of   network   traffic   identification   and 
management use tagging protocols such as IEEE 802.1Q. This way multiple VLANs can 
share the same physical medium while remaining logically separate.

Layer 3: Network Layer 
 
Manages  the  routing  of  data  packets  between  devices  across different  networks.  It  is  a 
connectionless  protocol,  which  means  that  the  recipient  does  not  know  that  someone  is 
sending those data and also there is no predetermined path that the data will follow from the 
sender to the recipient. Finally, the IP protocol is not involved with the proper transport of the 
packet, which is the responsibility of a higher protocol. 
The Network Layer deals with the Logical Addressing of the devices The Network Layer uses 
logical IP addressing to identify interfaces and route packets between networks. 
IP  addresses  may  be  configured  manually  or  assigned  through  mechanisms  such  as  DHCP, 
DHCPv6,  or  SLAAC  (Stateless  Address  Autoconfiguration).  This  layer  determines  the  most 
efficient path for data traffic from source to destination device. Since the path is determined 
the  next  thing  is  to  forward  traffic  from  one  network  to  another  until  the  data  reach  the 
receiver. Many times,  a packet  is too large  to be  transmitted over a network, so it must  be 
break down into smaller fragments to be easier to send it from one destination to another. As 
soon  as  the  data  packet  reaches  its  final  destination  the  Network  Layer  undertakes  to 
reassemble it into its original form. 
Each  Network  Interface  has  a  unique  IP  address. There  are  two  versions of  IP protocol,  the 
IPv4 and IPv6. Since 2012 IPv4 has been exhausted and we have moved to IPv6, but it is not 
that often used in internal networks at least.

Routing Protocols 
 
Routing protocols are important for defining the best route for data packets to travel across a 
network.  They  allow  routers  to  communicate  with  each  other,  share  information  about 
changes  in  network  topology,  and  make  decisions  on  the  most  efficient  routes  for  data 
transmission. 
Interior Gateway Protocols (IGP) 
 
Used in a single Autonomous System (AS), such as RIP, RIPv2, EIGRP, OSPF. 
Exterior Gateway Protocols (EGP) 
 
Used to route traffic between different Autonomous Systems, such as: BGP (Boarder Gateway 
Protocol). 
Autonomous System (AS) 
An autonomous system is the collection of connected networks that are managed by a single 
organization. Each AS has a unique number called Autonomous System Number (ASN), which 
is used by BGP in order to exchange routing information between different ASes. 
There are three types of Autonomous Systems: 
1. Stub AS: Connects to only one AS. 
2. Multi-Homed AS: Connects to multiple ASes without transit the information.  
3. Transit AS: Connects Multiple ASes but also allows data to pass through.

The protocols are divided into Distance Vector Protocols, Link State Protocols, and Path Vector 
Protocols. 
Distance Vector Routing Protocols 
They determine the best path based on hops, i.e. in how many hops will the information have 
to take to reach the destination. (RIP, IGRP cisco proprietary protocol) 
Link State Routing Protocols 
Have a complete view of the network topology and use algorithms to calculate the shortest 
(fastest) path (OSPF, IS-IS). 
Path Vector Routing Protocols 
Maintain  the  path  information  that  gets  updated  dynamically  as  the  network  topology 
changes (BGP).

Boarder Gateway Protocol (BGP) 
The  BGP  is  an  exterior  gateway  protocol  which  is  used  to  exchange  routing  information 
between different Autonomous Systems on the Internet. 
The way that the BGP works is as follows: 
o Peering: The BGP Routers (peers) establish a connection in order to exchange routing 
information.  
o Route Advertisement: The BGP Routers advertise the routes they know to the other 
already connected routers.  
o Path  Selection:  The  way  that  BGP  chooses  the  right  path is  based  on  many  metrics 
such as path length and policy rules. 
o Route Propagation: As soon as a router decides the best path then it propagates the 
path to the other peers to have the updated routing information.

Layer 4: Transport Layer 
The Transport Layer provides end-to-end communication between applications. 
Functions such as reliability, ordering, acknowledgements, retransmission, flow control, and 
connection management depend on the transport protocol. TCP provides these mechanisms, 
while UDP does not provide delivery guarantees.  
TCP Layer basic features: 
 End-to-end  Communication:  Offers  connectivity  between  source  and  destination 
device. 
 Segmentation  &  Reassembly:  It  breaks  big  data  streams  into  smaller  and  easier  to 
manage  data  segments.  At  the destination  point  transport  layer  is  obliged  to 
reassembly the data segments into their original form. 
 Connection  Management:  It  establishes,  maintains,  and  terminates  the  connection 
between communicating devices.  
 Flow Control: Controls the data transmission rate to avoid network congestion. 
 Error   Detection   &   Correction:   It   detects   errors   and   corrupted   data   must   be 
retransmitted. 
 Multiplexing: Multiple apps can use the network simultaneously. 
The TCP Layer has two main transmission protocols: 
1. TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) 
It checks the status of the connection and also checks whether the data was received correctly 
by the user. It is Full Duplex, meaning the data exchange takes place at the same time. 
Prior to data transmission the TCP must establish a connection between the two ends. This 
process  is  called 3-Way  Handshake. It’s a fundamental process used in the Transmission 
Control  Protocol  (TCP)  to  establish  a  reliable  connection  between  a  client  and  a  server,  in 
order to exchange data.  
 
3-way Handshake

3-way Handshake Procedure 
 SYN  packet:  The  client  sends  a  synchronization  packet  to  the  server  to  initiate  a 
connection.  
 SYN-ACK packet: The server responds to the client with an Acknowledge packet. 
 ACK  packet:  Finally  the  client  responds  to  server  with  an  Acknowledge  packet  and 
then the connection is established, and the two sides are ready to exchange data. 
TCP uses a sliding-window mechanism for flow control. 
The  receiver  advertises  how  much  additional  data  it  is  prepared  to  accept.  TCP  may  use 
cumulative    and    delayed    acknowledgements    and    does    not    necessarily    send    one 
acknowledgement for every received segment. 
Congestion control is a separate mechanism that adjusts transmission behavior according to 
conditions within the network. 
Finally, after the communication is completed and one of the two ends wishes to terminate 
the connection (client for example), sends a FIN packet, the recipient (server) responds with 
an  ACK  packet  and  at  the same  time  sends  another FIN  (ACK/FIN)  packet  and  the  side  that 
started (the client) it sends a final ACK packet, and the connection is terminated.

UDP (User Datagram Protocol) 
It is also a protocol of the fourth layer of the OSI Reference model and its purpose is 
transmission speed and not transmission reliability. 
The main features of UDP are: 
1. Connectionless:  Unlike  TCP,  UDP  does  not  set  up  communication  before 
sending data. Which makes it fast but not as reliable.  
2. No Guarantees: It has no mechanism to check that the package was delivered 
correctly or that it was delivered in the correct order. 
3. Low  Overhead:  Because  of  this  it  is  suitable  for  applications  where  speed  is 
essential. 
4. Usage: It is mainly used in applications that are time sensitive, such as video 
streaming, VoIP, DNS, Online Gaming.

Layer 5: Session Layer 
This layer is responsible for establishing, managing, and terminating sessions between 
applications.  
Key Features 
• Session Management: Establishes, maintains, and terminates communication 
sessions between applications. 
• Communication Control: Manages the communication between two devices, 
ensuring  that  data  is  properly  synchronized, and  the  two  devices  have  no 
problems in communication. 
• Data Synchronization: Provides synchronization in   data   traffic   between 
devices,  allowing for  recovery  and  continuation of  communication  in  case  of 
disruptions.

Layer 6: Presentation Layer 
This  layer  is  responsible  for  the  translation,  encryption,  and  compression  of  data.  Converts 
data from the application layer into a format that can be sent over the network. It encrypts 
data  before  transmission  and  decrypts  them  at  the  destination,  that  way  data  is  protected

order  to  reduce  the  size.  A  very  important  part  of  Layer  6  is  to  ensure  that  the  data  is 
presented in an understandable way from the receiving application.
